# Hypotension and Fluid Resuscitation in Sepsis
Source: Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## Recognising sepsis-induced hypoperfusion
Sepsis-induced hypoperfusion is suggested by hypotension (systolic pressure below 90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure below 65 mmHg), raised lactate, prolonged capillary refill, mottled skin, reduced urine output or altered mental state. Sepsis and septic shock are medical emergencies, and treatment and resuscitation should begin immediately.

## Initial fluid resuscitation
For patients with sepsis-induced hypoperfusion or septic shock, the guidelines suggest that at least 30 mL/kg of intravenous crystalloid be given within the first three hours of resuscitation. This is a weak recommendation based on low-quality evidence, and the volume should be individualised, for example in patients with heart failure, advanced kidney disease or signs of fluid overload.

## Choice of fluid
Crystalloids are recommended as first-line fluid. Balanced crystalloids are suggested instead of normal saline. Albumin is suggested in patients who have received large volumes of crystalloid. Starches are recommended against because of increased risk of kidney injury and death, and gelatin is suggested against.

## Dynamic assessment of fluid responsiveness
After initial resuscitation, further fluid should be guided by frequent reassessment of haemodynamic status. Dynamic measures such as passive leg raising with cardiac output measurement, stroke volume variation or pulse pressure variation are suggested over physical examination or static parameters alone. Giving fluid to non-responders risks oedema, worsened oxygenation and organ dysfunction.

## Mean arterial pressure target
For adults with septic shock on vasopressors, an initial target mean arterial pressure of 65 mmHg is recommended over higher targets. Higher targets have not improved survival in general populations and increase vasopressor exposure.

## Persistent hypotension
Hypotension that persists despite adequate fluid resuscitation defines a need for vasopressor therapy and is a key feature of septic shock. Persistent hypotension should also prompt a search for an uncontrolled infection source, cardiac dysfunction, bleeding, adrenal insufficiency or other causes of shock.
