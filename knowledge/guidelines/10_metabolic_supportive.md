# Acid-Base, Glucose and Supportive Care in Sepsis
Source: Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## Metabolic acidosis
Low pH, low bicarbonate and a negative base excess in sepsis usually reflect lactic acidosis from hypoperfusion, sometimes combined with renal dysfunction. Treatment targets the cause by restoring perfusion. Sodium bicarbonate is suggested against for hypoperfusion-induced lactic acidaemia in general, but is suggested for patients with severe metabolic acidaemia (pH 7.2 or lower) and acute kidney injury.

## Hyperglycaemia
Stress hyperglycaemia is common in sepsis. Insulin therapy is recommended when blood glucose is 180 mg/dL or higher, with a typical target range of 144 to 180 mg/dL. Tight glucose control with lower targets increases the risk of hypoglycaemia without clear benefit.

## Hyperkalaemia
Potassium can rise in sepsis-associated acute kidney injury, acidosis and tissue breakdown. A raised potassium needs ECG assessment and treatment of the cause, and severe or refractory hyperkalaemia with kidney injury is an indication to consider renal replacement therapy.

## Supportive care bundle
Other supportive measures for adults with sepsis include venous thromboembolism prophylaxis, stress ulcer prophylaxis in patients with risk factors for gastrointestinal bleeding, early enteral nutrition within 72 hours when feasible, and discussion of goals of care and prognosis with patients and families early in the course of illness.

## Reassessment and escalation
Patients flagged as high risk need frequent reassessment of vital signs, perfusion markers and organ function, and timely escalation to senior clinicians or critical care outreach. Documentation of the time sepsis was suspected supports quality improvement and audit.
