# Sepsis Definitions and Screening
Source: Singer M. et al., Sepsis-3 consensus definitions, JAMA 2016; Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## Sepsis-3 definition
Sepsis is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. Organ dysfunction is identified operationally as an acute increase in the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score of two points or more attributable to the infection. A baseline SOFA score of zero can be assumed in patients without known pre-existing organ dysfunction. An increase of two or more points is associated with an in-hospital mortality of roughly ten percent in a general hospital population with suspected infection.

## Septic shock definition
Septic shock is a subset of sepsis in which circulatory, cellular and metabolic abnormalities are profound enough to substantially increase mortality. Clinically it is identified by a vasopressor requirement to maintain a mean arterial pressure of 65 mmHg or higher together with a serum lactate level above 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation. Hospital mortality in septic shock exceeds forty percent.

## SOFA components
The SOFA score grades six organ systems from zero to four points: respiration (PaO2/FiO2 ratio and respiratory support), coagulation (platelet count), liver (bilirubin), cardiovascular (mean arterial pressure and vasopressor use), central nervous system (Glasgow Coma Scale) and renal function (creatinine or urine output). Falling platelet counts, rising bilirubin, rising creatinine and hypotension each add points and signal new organ dysfunction.

## Screening tools
Hospitals are advised to run performance improvement programmes that include sepsis screening for acutely ill, high-risk patients. The quick SOFA (qSOFA) score uses respiratory rate of 22 per minute or more, altered mentation and systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less. The 2021 guidance recommends against using qSOFA alone as a single screening tool compared with SIRS, NEWS or MEWS, because a negative qSOFA does not rule out sepsis. Screening tools identify patients who need further assessment; they do not establish the diagnosis.

## SIRS criteria in context
The systemic inflammatory response syndrome criteria are temperature above 38 °C or below 36 °C, heart rate above 90 per minute, respiratory rate above 20 per minute or PaCO2 below 32 mmHg, and white cell count above 12 or below 4 x10^3/uL. SIRS criteria are sensitive but not specific: many hospitalised patients meet them without infection, and some patients with sepsis do not. Under Sepsis-3 they are no longer part of the definition but remain useful as triggers for evaluation.

## Machine-learning early warning
Automated early-warning models analyse trends in vital signs and laboratory values to flag deterioration earlier than periodic manual scoring. Their output should prompt bedside clinical assessment rather than replace it, and alert thresholds must balance sensitivity against alarm fatigue. Model explanations that show which measurements drove an alert help clinicians judge whether the alert is plausible.
