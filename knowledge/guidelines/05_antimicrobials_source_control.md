# Antimicrobial Therapy and Source Control
Source: Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## Timing of antimicrobials
For adults with possible septic shock or a high likelihood of sepsis, antimicrobials are recommended immediately, ideally within one hour of recognition. For adults with possible sepsis without shock, a rapid assessment of infectious versus non-infectious causes is suggested, and if concern for infection persists, antimicrobials should be given within three hours of first recognising sepsis. For patients with low likelihood of infection and no shock, deferring antimicrobials while monitoring closely is suggested.

## Cultures before antibiotics
Appropriate microbiological cultures, including at least two sets of blood cultures, should be obtained before starting antimicrobials when this does not cause a meaningful delay. Cultures allow later narrowing of therapy.

## Empiric coverage
Empiric therapy should cover the likely pathogens based on the suspected source, local resistance patterns and patient risk factors. Coverage for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus is recommended in patients at high risk of MRSA. Using two Gram-negative agents is suggested only for patients at high risk of multidrug-resistant organisms. Empiric antifungal therapy is suggested only for patients at high risk of fungal infection.

## Procalcitonin and de-escalation
Procalcitonin together with clinical evaluation is suggested to help decide when to stop antimicrobials, but not to decide when to start them. Daily assessment for de-escalation is suggested, and shorter courses are suggested over longer ones when adequate source control has been achieved.

## Source control
A specific anatomical source of infection that needs emergent source control, such as an abscess, infected device, obstructed urinary tract or intra-abdominal perforation, should be identified or excluded rapidly. Any required source control intervention should be performed as soon as medically and logistically practical. Intravascular devices that may be the source should be removed promptly after other access is established.
