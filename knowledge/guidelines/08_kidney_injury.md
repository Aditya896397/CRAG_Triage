# Acute Kidney Injury in Sepsis
Source: KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury, Kidney Int Suppl 2012; Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2012
URL: https://kdigo.org/guidelines/acute-kidney-injury/

## Definition and staging
Acute kidney injury (AKI) is defined by any of: a rise in serum creatinine of 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours; a rise to 1.5 times baseline or more within the previous seven days; or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/h for six hours. Stage 2 corresponds to creatinine 2.0 to 2.9 times baseline or low urine output for twelve hours, and stage 3 to three times baseline, creatinine of 4.0 mg/dL or more, initiation of renal replacement therapy, or anuria for twelve hours.

## Sepsis-associated AKI
Sepsis is the most common cause of AKI in the ICU. Mechanisms include hypoperfusion, inflammation and microvascular dysfunction. A rising creatinine or blood urea nitrogen in a patient with suspected infection indicates renal dysfunction and contributes to the SOFA score.

## Management principles
Management focuses on restoring perfusion, avoiding nephrotoxic drugs and contrast where possible, adjusting drug doses to renal function, and monitoring fluid balance and electrolytes such as potassium. Hyperkalaemia, severe acidosis, fluid overload and uraemic complications are indications to consider renal replacement therapy.

## Renal replacement therapy in sepsis
For adults with sepsis or septic shock and AKI who need renal replacement therapy, either continuous or intermittent therapy is suggested. Renal replacement therapy is suggested against in sepsis-associated AKI when the only abnormality is a raised creatinine or low urine output without definitive indications.
