# Leukocyte, Platelet, Coagulation and Liver Abnormalities in Sepsis
Source: Singer M. et al., Sepsis-3, JAMA 2016; Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2492881

## Leukocytosis and leukopenia
A white cell count above 12 x10^3/uL (leukocytosis) or below 4 x10^3/uL (leukopenia) is a SIRS criterion. Leukopenia in sepsis may indicate overwhelming infection, bone marrow suppression or immunosuppression and is associated with poor outcomes. Leukocytosis is non-specific and can follow surgery, steroids or stress.

## Thrombocytopenia
A platelet count below 150 x10^3/uL is common in sepsis and contributes to the coagulation component of the SOFA score. Falling platelets may reflect consumption, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), marrow suppression, drug effects such as heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, or dilution. Progressive thrombocytopenia is associated with higher mortality and should prompt a coagulation screen.

## Coagulopathy and DIC
Sepsis can activate coagulation and impair fibrinolysis, leading to DIC with prolonged clotting times, low fibrinogen and microvascular thrombosis. Management centres on treating the underlying infection. Pharmacological venous thromboembolism prophylaxis is recommended for adults with sepsis unless contraindicated, with low molecular weight heparin preferred over unfractionated heparin.

## Hepatic dysfunction
A total bilirubin above 1.2 mg/dL indicates liver dysfunction in the SOFA score. Sepsis-associated liver injury may present as cholestasis or as hypoxic hepatitis with very high transaminases in shock. Raised bilirubin in sepsis is associated with worse outcomes and may also reduce lactate clearance.

## Anaemia and transfusion
For adults with sepsis or septic shock, a restrictive transfusion strategy is recommended over a liberal one, typically transfusing red cells when haemoglobin falls below 7 g/dL in the absence of myocardial ischaemia, severe hypoxaemia or acute bleeding.
