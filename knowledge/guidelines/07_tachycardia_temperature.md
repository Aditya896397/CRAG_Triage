# Tachycardia, Fever and Hypothermia in Suspected Sepsis
Source: Singer M. et al., Sepsis-3, JAMA 2016; Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2492881

## Tachycardia
A heart rate above 90 per minute is a SIRS criterion and often accompanies infection, fever, hypovolaemia, pain or anaemia. In sepsis, persistent tachycardia despite fluid resuscitation and fever control may indicate ongoing hypoperfusion, uncontrolled infection or new arrhythmia such as atrial fibrillation. Tachycardia combined with hypotension and raised lactate strongly suggests shock and requires urgent evaluation.

## Evaluating tachycardia in the ICU
Evaluation should include a review of volume status and fluid responsiveness, a 12-lead ECG to exclude arrhythmia, assessment of pain and agitation, review of medications such as inotropes, and consideration of bleeding or pulmonary embolism. Treating the underlying cause is preferred over rate control in septic tachycardia.

## Fever
A temperature above 38 °C in an ICU patient should prompt assessment for infection, including examination of lines and wounds, blood cultures and targeted imaging. Not all fever is infective: drug reactions, thromboembolism, transfusion and inflammatory conditions can also raise temperature. Fever increases metabolic demand and heart rate.

## Hypothermia
A temperature below 36 °C in suspected sepsis is associated with worse outcomes than fever and may indicate a failing host response. Hypothermic septic patients may show fewer classic signs of infection, so a low temperature should not be reassuring. Hypothermia also impairs coagulation and immune function.

## Combining vital-sign abnormalities
Single vital-sign abnormalities are common and non-specific. The combination of several abnormalities, and their trend over time, is more informative for predicting deterioration. Early warning scores aggregate heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, temperature, oxygen saturation and consciousness for this reason.
