# Tachypnoea, Hypoxaemia and Respiratory Support in Sepsis
Source: Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## Tachypnoea as an early sign
A raised respiratory rate is one of the earliest and most sensitive signs of physiological deterioration. It may reflect hypoxaemia, metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation (for example from raised lactate), pain, fever or a primary pulmonary infection. A respiratory rate of 22 per minute or more is part of the qSOFA score, and more than 20 per minute is a SIRS criterion.

## Hypoxaemia assessment
Low oxygen saturation in a septic patient may indicate pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), pulmonary oedema from fluid overload, or reduced oxygen delivery from shock. Arterial blood gas analysis gives the PaO2/FiO2 ratio, which grades respiratory dysfunction in the SOFA score and in ARDS. A rising oxygen requirement (FiO2) is itself a marker of worsening lung function.

## High-flow nasal oxygen
For adults with sepsis-induced hypoxaemic respiratory failure, high-flow nasal oxygen is suggested over non-invasive ventilation. Patients on either form of support need close monitoring so that intubation is not delayed if they deteriorate.

## Lung-protective ventilation
For adults with sepsis-induced ARDS, a low tidal volume strategy of about 6 mL/kg predicted body weight is recommended over higher tidal volumes, with an upper limit goal for plateau pressure of 30 cmH2O. Higher PEEP is suggested for moderate to severe ARDS, and prone positioning for more than twelve hours daily is recommended for moderate to severe ARDS.

## Conservative fluid strategy after resuscitation
Once shock has resolved, excessive positive fluid balance worsens oxygenation. Ongoing fluid therapy should therefore be guided by reassessment of fluid responsiveness, and patients with hypoxaemia should not receive fluid without evidence that it will improve perfusion.
