# Vasopressors and Corticosteroids in Septic Shock
Source: Evans L. et al., Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines, Crit Care Med 2021
Year: 2021
URL: https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines-2021

## First-line vasopressor
Norepinephrine is recommended as the first-line vasopressor in adults with septic shock, in preference to other agents. It raises mean arterial pressure mainly through vasoconstriction with modest effects on heart rate, and has been associated with fewer arrhythmias than dopamine.

## Adding a second agent
For patients on norepinephrine with inadequate mean arterial pressure, adding vasopressin is suggested rather than escalating norepinephrine further. In practice vasopressin is usually started when the norepinephrine dose is in the range of 0.25 to 0.5 micrograms per kilogram per minute. If pressure remains inadequate despite norepinephrine and vasopressin, adding epinephrine is suggested.

## Cardiac dysfunction
In septic shock with cardiac dysfunction and persistent hypoperfusion despite adequate volume status and arterial pressure, adding dobutamine to norepinephrine or switching to epinephrine is suggested. Bedside echocardiography helps identify myocardial depression.

## Peripheral administration and arterial monitoring
Vasopressors may be started through a peripheral vein rather than delaying until central venous access is obtained, with close monitoring of the site. An arterial catheter is suggested for continuous blood pressure monitoring as soon as practical in patients requiring vasopressors.

## Corticosteroids
For adults with septic shock and an ongoing requirement for vasopressor therapy, intravenous corticosteroids are suggested. The typical trigger is norepinephrine or epinephrine at 0.25 micrograms per kilogram per minute or more for at least four hours after initiation. Corticosteroids shorten the duration of shock but have not consistently reduced mortality.
